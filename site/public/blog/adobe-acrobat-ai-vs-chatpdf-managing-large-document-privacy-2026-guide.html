Adobe Acrobat AI Assistant: privacy controls for PDFs in Acrobat

If you’re evaluating Adobe Acrobat AI vs ChatPDF: Managing Large Document Privacy, start with how Adobe frames enterprise privacy. Adobe positions Acrobat AI Assistant for enterprise-grade security and privacy controls.

Adobe also states it does not use customer data to train or fine-tune LLMs, and it limits how its LLM service is handled. For privacy-heavy departments, that matters more than clever prompts.

Document-grounded responses (less “random chat”)

By default, Adobe says AI Assistant grounds its responses in the content of the document you open (or explicitly select). That reduces the risk of answers drifting away from the actual contract, policy, or report you uploaded.

What gets deleted, and when

For Adobe Acrobat AI vs ChatPDF: Managing Large Document Privacy, the clearest operational detail is the deletion window. Adobe says uploaded documents, prompts, and generated responses are automatically deleted after 12 hours, with retention only for chat history and user-reported content.

Where chat history lives (device vs cloud)

Adobe further breaks it down by deployment: on desktop and mobile, chat history is stored on the user’s device, while the web version stores it in Adobe’s cloud.

That distinction is a big deal for large-document privacy, because web access changes the retention and exposure surface compared to device-based storage.

Admin controls for teams

For orgs rolling out ai tools across roles, Adobe also describes an Admin Console setting to turn off cloud-based chat history storage for the organization. That’s the kind of switch you need when ai automation becomes business process, not just personal use.
