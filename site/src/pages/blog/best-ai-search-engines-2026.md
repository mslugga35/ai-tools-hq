## How Traditional Search vs. AI Search Actually Differs

Traditional search gives you 10 blue links and makes you do the reading. AI search reads the sources for you, synthesizes an answer, and cites where it got the information.

The tradeoff: AI search is better for questions with clear answers ("What's the difference between OLED and Mini-LED?") but worse for browsing, shopping, and discovery where you want to see many options side by side. Most people will use both — AI search for answers, traditional search for exploration.

**What to watch for in every AI search engine:**

1. **Source citations** — Does it show where the answer came from? Can you click through to verify?
2. **Freshness** — Is it searching the live web or answering from training data?
3. **Model access** — Which AI models power it? Can you choose?
4. **Privacy** — Does it track your queries? Use them for ads? Train on your data?
5. **Cost** — Free tiers are shrinking. What do you actually get without paying?

- No opt-out — AI Overviews appear whether you want them or not

**Who it's for:** Everyone who doesn't actively choose an alternative. If you're reading this article, you're probably ready to try something else — but Google AI Overviews remains the default that billions use without thinking about it.

- $10/mo for search feels expensive when Google and Perplexity both have free tiers

**Who it's for:** Developers, journalists, researchers, and privacy advocates who accept that good search costs money. ## What Happened to Phind and Arc Search?

Two notable AI search products didn't survive to mid-2026:

**Phind** — the developer-focused AI search engine — **shut down on January 16, 2026**, barely a month after raising $10 million in funding. No advance warning was given to users. Phind had carved a niche with its 32K token context window and Phind-70B model optimized for technical queries, but it couldn't sustain the economics of running inference at scale. Former Phind users have largely migrated to Perplexity (for AI search) and Cursor or GitHub Copilot (for in-IDE coding assistance).

**Arc Search** by The Browser Company was acquired by Atlassian for $610 million in May 2025 and immediately entered maintenance mode. The browser is still downloadable and receives Chromium security patches (most recently v1.147.1 on May 22, 2026), but no new features are being developed. The Browser Company's focus shifted entirely to Dia, an AI-first browser. If you're still using Arc, it works fine — but it's frozen in amber.

## The Copyright Problem No One Has Solved

Every AI search engine faces the same fundamental tension: they need to read publisher content to generate answers, but publishers argue this is copyright infringement.

The lawsuit count in 2026:
- **CNN vs. Perplexity** (May 2026) — alleging scraping of 17,000+ stories
- **BBC, NYT, Reddit** vs. Perplexity — various copyright claims
- **Japanese publishers** (Yomiuri, Asahi, Nikkei) vs. Perplexity
- **News Corp vs. Brave Search** (July 2026) — alleging disguised crawlers
- **NYT vs. OpenAI/Microsoft** (December 2023, still pending)

Some companies are licensing instead of litigating: Perplexity has revenue-sharing deals with Time magazine and the LA Times. The industry is slowly moving toward a licensing model, but the legal landscape remains unresolved.

**What this means for users:** The AI search engine you use today might change how it handles sources tomorrow. Favor tools that cite sources transparently — if a tool can show you where it got the information, it's more likely to survive the legal reckoning.

## FAQ

### Are AI search engines more accurate than Google?

For specific factual questions, yes — AI search engines synthesize multiple sources into a direct answer, which is often faster and more accurate than scanning 10 blue links. For ambiguous queries, shopping, or local search, Google's traditional results are still better. The key difference: AI search engines cite their sources, so you can verify claims. Google AI Overviews often don't make it clear where information came from.

### Is Perplexity AI worth $20/month?

If you search for information professionally — research, writing, analysis, decision-making — yes. The free tier is too limited for daily use, but Pro gives you unlimited queries, frontier model selection (GPT-5.2, Claude 4.6), Model Council for comparing answers, and Deep Research. At $20/mo, it replaces the time you'd spend clicking through Google results and reading individual articles. If you search casually (a few times a day), the free tier or Andi Search is probably enough.

### What happened to Phind?

Phind, the developer-focused AI search engine, shut down on January 16, 2026 — roughly one month after its last funding round. No warning was given to users. The closest alternatives are Perplexity AI (general AI search with code understanding), Cursor (AI-assisted coding in IDE), and GitHub Copilot (inline code completion and chat).

### Can AI search engines replace Google entirely?

Not yet. AI search engines excel at answering specific questions but struggle with browsing-style searches (shopping comparisons, image search, local business discovery, maps). Most power users run an AI search engine alongside Google rather than replacing it entirely. The gap is closing — Perplexity's Comet browser and ChatGPT's Agent Mode are both pushing toward full browsing replacement — but we're not there in 2026.

### Which AI search engine is best for students?

Andi Search (completely free, no account needed) for casual use. Perplexity AI (free tier) for research with cited sources — the citations are particularly valuable for academic work because you can trace every claim back to a source. Avoid relying solely on ChatGPT for research papers — it's better at generating text than at accurately citing verifiable sources.
