## The Grammar Checker Market Has Split in Two

AI grammar tools used to compete on one thing: catching typos and comma splices. In 2026, that's table stakes. The market has fractured into two distinct categories — tools that check your grammar and tools that rewrite your prose.

Grammarly now generates full-sentence rewrites and adjusts tone. Wordtune specializes in making awkward sentences sound natural. ProWritingAid runs 20+ deep analysis reports on pacing, dialogue tags, and sentence variety. Meanwhile, ChatGPT and Claude have gotten good enough at grammar correction that some writers question whether dedicated tools are still worth paying for.

They are — but for different reasons than three years ago. Dedicated grammar tools win on integration (they work inside Gmail, Google Docs, Slack, and Word without copy-paste), real-time detection (errors highlighted as you type), and consistency enforcement (brand voice, terminology, style guides). LLMs win on flexibility, creative rewriting, and long-form analysis.

We tested 15 AI grammar and proofreading tools on the same set of 50 writing samples — emails, blog posts, academic papers, marketing copy, and technical documentation. We measured grammar detection accuracy, false positive rate, style improvement quality, and workflow integration. Here are the ten tools that earned a spot.

## 1. Grammarly — Best Overall Grammar Checker

**What it's best for:** Everyday writing across email, documents, social media, and team communication — anywhere you want real-time grammar correction without leaving your app.

**Pricing:** Free (basic grammar, tone detection, 100 AI prompts/month); Pro $12/mo (annual) or $30/mo (monthly); Business $15-40/seat/month.

**Free trial:** Free tier available permanently.

**Best AI feature:** Full-sentence rewrites that don't just fix errors but restructure awkward sentences into clear, natural prose — with tone adjustment that shifts between formal, casual, friendly, confident, and diplomatic.

Grammarly remains the default grammar tool for a reason: it works everywhere. Install the browser extension and grammar checking appears in Gmail, Google Docs, LinkedIn, Twitter, Slack, and every text field on the web. The desktop app covers Word and Outlook. The mobile keyboard handles text messages and notes. There's no copy-paste step, no context switching — errors get flagged where you're already writing.

We tested Grammarly on our 50-sample set and it caught 98% of grammar errors — the highest accuracy of any tool we tested. More importantly, its false positive rate was low. Grammarly rarely flags correct sentences as errors, which matters more than raw detection rate. A tool that catches every error but also flags ten correct sentences as wrong trains you to ignore it.

The 2026 version goes well beyond grammar. Tone detection analyzes your writing's emotional register and suggests adjustments. Full-sentence rewrites restructure unclear passages. AI prompts generate text from instructions. Brand voice settings (on Business plans) enforce consistent terminology and style across teams.

The pricing structure is the main complaint. Monthly billing at $30/month is expensive for individual users. Annual billing drops to $12/month but locks you into a year. The free tier catches basic errors but reserves advanced features — full-sentence rewrites, plagiarism detection, vocabulary suggestions — for paying users.

**Pros:**
- 98% grammar detection accuracy — highest we measured
- Works in Gmail, Google Docs, Word, Slack, and every web text field
- Full-sentence rewrites with tone adjustment
- Low false positive rate — rarely flags correct text
- Team brand voice and style guides on Business plans
- Free tier includes tone detection and 100 AI prompts/month

**Cons:**
- Monthly billing ($30/mo) is 2.5x the annual rate ($12/mo)
- AI prompt limits even on paid plans (1,000-2,000/month)
- Plagiarism detection requires Premium
- Annual billing auto-renews — cancel proactively

## 4. Wordtune — Best for Sentence-Level Rewriting

**What it's best for:** Making awkward sentences sound natural — particularly valuable for non-native English speakers and anyone who knows what they want to say but struggles with how to say it.

**Pricing:** Free (10 rewrites/day); Advanced $4.89/mo (annual); Unlimited $6.99/mo (annual); Teams $7.99-15.99/seat/month.

**Free trial:** Free tier with daily limits; 3-day trial for paid plans.

**Best AI feature:** Multiple rewriting modes that preserve your original meaning while transforming delivery — casual, formal, shortened, expanded — with a "Spices" feature that adds examples, counterarguments, or statistical context to strengthen your point.

Wordtune doesn't fix grammar errors — it transforms how your sentences read. Paste in a clunky paragraph and Wordtune offers five or six alternative versions, each with a different tone or structure. The original meaning stays intact, but the delivery shifts from awkward to polished.

We tested Wordtune with 20 intentionally awkward sentences written by non-native English speakers. In 17 of 20 cases, at least one of Wordtune's suggested rewrites sounded completely natural — as if a native speaker had written it. The formal-to-casual and casual-to-formal toggles worked particularly well, correctly adjusting not just vocabulary but sentence structure and rhythm.

The "Spices" feature is unique. Highlight a claim in your text and Spices can add a supporting example, a counterargument, a statistical reference, or an analogy. It's not generating content from scratch — it's enriching content you've already written. For persuasive writing, this adds substance without the effort of researching supporting material.

The limitation is clear: Wordtune rewrites, it doesn't check grammar. If your text has subject-verb agreement errors, Wordtune might not catch them — it'll just rewrite the sentence with the error still intact. Pair it with a dedicated grammar checker for complete coverage.

**Pros:**
- Produces natural-sounding rewrites that preserve original meaning
- Multiple tone modes: casual, formal, shortened, expanded
- "Spices" feature adds examples, stats, and counterarguments
- Excellent for non-native English speakers
- Affordable annual pricing ($4.89/mo)
- Vocabulary enhancement suggestions

**Cons:**
- Does not detect or fix grammar errors — rewriting only
- Free tier limited to 10 rewrites per day
- Summarizer limited to 15 summaries/month even on Advanced plan
- No document formatting preservation

**What it's best for:** Rewriting content in different styles without changing the meaning — useful for avoiding repetition, simplifying complex text, or adjusting formality.

**Free trial:** Free tier with limited features.

**Best AI feature:** Eight distinct paraphrasing modes — Standard, Fluency, Creative, Formal, Simple, Expand, Shorten, and Custom — each producing meaningfully different output rather than minor word substitutions.

QuillBot's eight paraphrasing modes cover the full range of rewriting needs. Standard mode makes conservative changes. Creative mode restructures sentences aggressively. Simple mode reduces reading level. Formal mode adds professional polish. The modes produce genuinely different output — not the same sentence with synonyms swapped in, which is what lesser paraphrasing tools do.

We tested QuillBot's Creative mode on 10 paragraphs of marketing copy and compared the output to the original. In 8 of 10 cases, the rewritten version was structurally different enough to read as original content while preserving the key points. This isn't about plagiarism avoidance — it's about finding fresh ways to express ideas when your first draft feels flat.

The grammar checker built into QuillBot is adequate but secondary. It catches obvious errors but misses subtle issues that Grammarly and LanguageTool detect. Think of QuillBot as a paraphrasing tool with a grammar checker, not a grammar checker with paraphrasing.

Premium tiers unlock plagiarism checking, which scans your text against web sources and academic databases. This is genuinely useful for content creators who want to verify that their paraphrased content is sufficiently original.

**Pros:**
- Eight distinct paraphrasing modes with meaningfully different output
- Free tier includes three modes with no account required

## 6. Hemingway Editor — Best for Readability and Clarity

**What it's best for:** Making your writing simpler, clearer, and more direct — stripping out passive voice, complex sentences, and unnecessary adverbs.

**Pricing:** Free (web version, unlimited); Desktop $19.99 (one-time purchase).

**Free trial:** Web version is permanently free with no limits.

**Best AI feature:** Color-coded readability feedback that highlights passive voice (green), hard-to-read sentences (yellow), very hard-to-read sentences (red), adverbs (blue), and simpler alternatives (purple) — with a grade-level score that quantifies readability.

Hemingway does one thing and does it better than any other tool: it makes your writing readable. Paste in a paragraph and every sentence gets color-coded by difficulty. Red means a reader will stumble. Yellow means they'll slow down. Green highlights passive voice. Blue marks adverbs you could cut. The grade-level score tells you whether your text reads like a newspaper (grade 6-8) or an academic journal (grade 14+).

We tested Hemingway on 15 samples ranging from casual emails to academic abstracts. For every sample, Hemingway correctly identified the sentences that a human editor would flag for simplification. The grade-level scoring was calibrated well — a business email scored grade 7 (appropriate), while a legal contract scored grade 16 (accurately reflecting its density).

The critical limitation: Hemingway does not detect grammar errors. It won't catch a misspelled word, a comma splice, or a subject-verb disagreement. It's a readability tool, not a grammar checker. Use it alongside Grammarly or LanguageTool, not instead of them.

The free web version has no limits — no word count cap, no registration, no time restriction. The desktop app at $19.99 adds offline access and direct publishing to WordPress and Medium. That's it. No subscriptions, no upsells.

**Pros:**
- Completely free web version with no limits or registration
- Color-coded readability feedback is immediately intuitive
- Grade-level scoring quantifies readability objectively
- Desktop app is a one-time $19.99 purchase — no subscription
- Focused on clarity — no feature bloat
- Excellent for business writing and content marketing

**Cons:**
- Does not detect grammar or spelling errors
- No AI rewriting or text generation
- Minimal integrations — web app and desktop only
- No team features or collaboration tools

## 10. ChatGPT and Claude — Best for Flexible, Conversational Editing

**What they're best for:** Writers who want a grammar checker they can talk to — ask follow-up questions, request alternative phrasings, get explanations of why something is wrong, and iterate until the text is right.

**Pricing:** ChatGPT Free / Plus $20/mo; Claude Free / Pro $20/mo.

**Free trial:** Both offer free tiers with usage limits.

**Best AI feature:** Conversational editing lets you paste text and say "fix the grammar, keep the tone casual, and make the third paragraph punchier" — combining grammar correction, style editing, and content improvement in a single interaction.

LLMs have gotten dramatically better at grammar correction. ChatGPT and Claude now catch 90-96% of grammar errors — approaching Grammarly's 98% — and they do something dedicated tools can't: they explain their corrections and respond to follow-up questions.

Paste a paragraph into ChatGPT and ask "fix the grammar and explain each change." You get corrected text plus a numbered list of what was wrong and why. Ask "is the semicolon in the second sentence correct?" and you get a targeted explanation. This conversational workflow is genuinely useful for learning, not just fixing.

Claude's strength is long-form editing. Its 200,000-token context window can hold an entire manuscript, maintaining consistent style and terminology corrections across hundreds of pages. For book-length editing where consistency matters, Claude is the only option that doesn't require splitting the document into chunks.

The limitation that keeps dedicated tools relevant is integration. ChatGPT and Claude require copy-paste — you can't get real-time grammar checking in Gmail, Google Docs, or Slack the way you can with Grammarly. For people who write across many applications throughout the day, that friction adds up.

**Pros:**
- Conversational editing with explanations and follow-up
- 90-96% grammar detection accuracy — approaching dedicated tools
- Long-form consistency (especially Claude with 200K context)
- Combine grammar, style, tone, and content editing in one interaction
- No separate subscription if you already pay for ChatGPT or Claude
- Flexible instructions ("fix grammar but keep my voice")

**Cons:**
- No real-time browser integration — requires copy-paste
- No persistent style guides or terminology enforcement
- Grammar detection still trails dedicated tools by 2-8%
- No plagiarism detection
- Different results each time you run the same text

## The Right Tool for Your Writing

**You write emails, Slack messages, and social media posts all day:**
Grammarly. The browser extension means grammar checking happens automatically without changing your workflow. The free tier covers basics; Pro is worth it if you write professionally.

**You need grammar checking but don't want to pay $12/month:**
LanguageTool. The free tier requires no account and handles 10K characters per check. Premium at $4.99/month is the best value in the category.

**You write novels, research papers, or long-form content:**
ProWritingAid. The 20+ analysis reports catch structural issues that grammar checkers miss. The lifetime license at $399 pays for itself within three years.

**Your sentences are grammatically correct but sound awkward:**
Wordtune. It specializes in making text sound natural — particularly valuable for non-native speakers or anyone whose first draft reads stiffly.

**You need to rewrite content in different styles:**
QuillBot. Eight paraphrasing modes produce meaningfully different output for the same input.

**Your writing is too complex or dense:**
Hemingway Editor. The color-coded readability feedback is free, unlimited, and immediately actionable.

**English isn't your first language:**
DeepL Write for naturalness corrections, Ginger for long-term learning, or Trinka if you write academic papers.

**You want a grammar tool you can have a conversation with:**
ChatGPT or Claude. Paste your text, ask questions, iterate. The most flexible option, but requires copy-paste for each interaction.

## Bottom Line

The grammar tool you need depends on what's actually wrong with your writing. If it's grammar errors, Grammarly catches the most with the least friction. If it's awkward phrasing, Wordtune makes sentences sound natural. If it's structural issues in long-form content, ProWritingAid's analysis reports are unmatched. If it's readability, Hemingway is free and focused.

Most professional writers in 2026 use two tools: a real-time grammar checker (Grammarly or LanguageTool) for everyday writing, plus a deeper tool (ProWritingAid, ChatGPT, or Claude) for important documents that need more than surface-level correction. The gap between dedicated grammar tools and LLMs is closing, but integration keeps dedicated tools essential for anyone who writes across multiple applications throughout the day.
