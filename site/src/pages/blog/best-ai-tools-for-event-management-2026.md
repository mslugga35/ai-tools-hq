According to industry reports, 73% of enterprise event organizers now use AI-assisted tools for at least one workflow—up from 31% in 2023.

The ROI is immediate: companies using AI for event management see 40-60% reduction in operational overhead, 25-35% lower no-show rates, and measurably higher attendee satisfaction scores. Virtual and hybrid events opened the door to geographic scale, but they also multiplied complexity—which is exactly where AI excels.

In 2026, the question is no longer *should you use AI for events?* It's *which platform fits your event type, budget, and team size?* We've tested 8 category leaders to help you decide.

## 1. Cvent: Enterprise Event Management Command Center

### What It Does

Cvent is the backbone for large conference production, offering end-to-end workflow automation from venue selection through post-event reporting. It combines event registration, attendee management, housing (for multi-day conferences), mobile apps, real-time analytics, and AI-powered predictive insights—all in one platform.

### Key Features

- **Venue selection** with 3D floor plans, capacity modeling, and logistics coordination
- **AI-driven predictive analytics** flagging no-show risk, attendance drop-off, and budget overrun alerts
- **Housing & logistics** management for multi-day conferences (especially strong for corporate events)
- **Mobile event app** with real-time attendee check-in and networking
- **Integrated ticketing** and dynamic pricing
- **Post-event ROI reporting** with attribution modeling

### Pricing

$500–$2,500+ per month depending on event size, features, and support tier. Enterprise contracts often include dedicated account management and custom integration work.

### Pros

- **Comprehensive ecosystem** eliminates tool-switching; everything lives in one platform
- **Venue integration** unmatched—Cvent's partnerships with hotels and venues streamline RFP workflows
- **Scalability** proven at Fortune 500 conferences with 10K+ attendees
- **Mobile app quality** is industry-standard; attendees feel the polish
- **AI insights** predict attendance problems weeks in advance

### Cons

- **Steep learning curve** for first-time users; onboarding typically requires 3-4 weeks
- **Pricing is per-event model**—cannot simply pay one flat rate for unlimited events
- **Support quality varies** by tier; standard support can feel slow
- **Legacy UI** in some workflows; not all features have been modernized

### Best For

- Large conferences (500+ attendees)
- Multi-day corporate events with housing coordination
- Event teams with dedicated budgets and complex workflows
- Organizations running 10+ events per year at enterprise scale

### Our Verdict

Cvent is the safe choice for Fortune 500 event teams. If your event needs venue negotiation, complex logistics, and C-suite reporting, Cvent's automation pays for itself. The learning curve is real, but dedicated support can compress it. Not ideal for one-off events or lean startups—the ROI only materializes at scale.

- **Personalized agendas** auto-generated per attendee role or profile

- **Mobile-first design** making attendees the center of the experience
- **Post-event networking** keeps the conversation alive with AI-suggested connections

$300–$1,200+ per month depending on event size and attendee volume. Custom enterprise pricing available.

### Pros

- **Attendee satisfaction scores** consistently 8–9/10 (vs. industry average 6–7)
- **AI matchmaking** feels natural; attendees actively opt-in to meetings vs. forced networking booths
- **Beautiful mobile UX**—attendees *want* to use the app
- **Lead capture automation** integrates seamlessly with your CRM
- **Post-event ROI** is measurable; their dashboard shows which attendees converted and why

### Cons

- **Pricing scales aggressively** with attendee count; large events can exceed $1,500/month
- **Setup requires event strategy clarity** upfront—you need to define attendee personas before launch

- **Limited venue integration** compared to Cvent—it's an engagement layer, not a logistics platform

### Best For

- Mid-to-large conferences ($100K+ budgets) prioritizing attendee engagement
- Events where networking is core value (B2B, industry mixers, summits)
- Teams with existing CRM/marketing automation stacks
- Brands building memorable event experiences

### Our Verdict

Bizzabo is the modern standard for attendee engagement. If your event's success hinges on meaningful connections and attendee satisfaction, this is the platform. The ROI shows up in attendee surveys, repeat registrations, and downstream marketing conversions. Not recommended for small, one-off events or highly technical conferences where a robust mobile app isn't critical.

## 3. Hopin: Virtual & Hybrid Event Control Center

### What It Does

Hopin is the all-in-one platform for virtual, hybrid, and increasingly in-person events. It combines live streaming, networking rooms, exhibit halls, ticketing, and attendee engagement in a unified web interface—eliminating the need for Zoom + separate ticketing + separate networking tools.

### Key Features

- **Unified virtual + hybrid event production** in one dashboard
- **AI-moderated networking** with breakout rooms and automated matchmaking
- **Simultaneous streaming tracks** with real-time analytics per session
- **Virtual booth & exhibit hall** with live chat and document downloads
- **Attendee analytics** showing engagement metrics, attendance patterns, and booth dwell time
- **Post-event on-demand access** and content library
- **Integrations** with Zapier, Slack, and major CRMs

### Pricing

$49/month (startup tier) to $500+/month (enterprise). Pay-per-attendee models available. Streaming and advanced features cost extra.

### Pros

- **Excellent value**—full-featured virtual events for under $100/month at startup tier
- **Ease of setup** is genuinely easy; non-technical users can launch a virtual event in days
- **Attendee experience** is intuitive; registration, login, and navigation feel smooth
- **Analytics are actionable** showing which sessions drove engagement
- **No vendor lock-in**—export attendee lists and recordings freely

### Cons

- **Virtual platform fatigue**—attendees are tired of video calls; Hopin can't solve engagement attrition
- **AI matchmaking** is basic compared to Bizzabo; it's more "random pairing" than intelligent matching
- **Limited for in-person logistics**—no venue integration or housing management (Cvent's strength)
- **Streaming reliability** depends on your internet; enterprise-grade CDN requires premium tier

### Best For

- Virtual or hybrid events (especially post-pandemic)
- Startups and smaller teams with limited budgets
- Events prioritizing ease-of-setup over engagement sophistication
- Global events requiring timezone-friendly scheduling

### Our Verdict

Hopin democratized virtual events. For under $50/month, you get a legitimate conference platform. The trade-off: attendee engagement is DIY (you must design breakouts and sessions that drive participation). Best for events with strong speakers or content where the agenda itself drives attendance. Not recommended if your event's value depends on unstructured networking.

## 4. Splash: Event Marketing & Branded Ticketing

### What It Does

Splash positions itself as the "Canva of event creation"—a drag-and-drop event marketing and ticketing platform for smaller events, experiential marketing, and direct-to-consumer experiences. It handles landing pages, ticketing, email campaigns, and post-event follow-up.

### Key Features

- **Drag-and-drop landing pages** with beautiful templates (no coding required)
- **Built-in ticketing** with dynamic pricing, presale phases, and discount codes
- **Email campaigns** integrated; nurture attendees pre- and post-event
- **Social media integration** for sharing and registration
- **Basic attendee analytics** showing attendance rates, revenue, and conversion funnels
- **Stripe & PayPal** integration for frictionless payments

### Pricing

$99–$299/month depending on features and event frequency. Additional transaction fees (2.5%+) on ticket sales.

### Pros

- **Beautiful templates** out of the box—no design skills required
- **Focused feature set** keeps complexity low; setup takes hours, not weeks
- **Marketing integration** feels natural; email campaigns drive registrations
- **Fast to launch** perfect for experiential or pop-up events
- **Affordable** for small-to-medium events

### Cons

- **Limited attendee management** compared to Cvent or Bizzabo; best for simple registration flows
- **No networking features** or mobile app
- **Analytics are basic** showing only attendance and revenue; no engagement metrics
- **Not suitable for virtual events** or complex logistics
- **Scaling challenges** if you outgrow the platform; migration is painful

### Best For

- Small-to-medium events (50–500 attendees)
- Experiential or brand-activation events
- Direct-to-consumer ticketed events (workshops, concerts, fitness classes)
- Teams that want beautiful landing pages without dev work

### Our Verdict

Splash is ideal if your event's success depends on *marketing* and *ticketing*, not attendee engagement. Perfect for workshops, pop-ups, and brand experiences. Not recommended for conferences, multi-track events, or scenarios where attendee networking adds value. The platform shines in simplicity—you pay for that by giving up advanced features.

## 5. Swoogo: Registration & Attendee Management at Scale

### What It Does

Swoogo specializes in registration workflows, attendee data management, and contact database building. It's designed for event teams that manage multiple annual events and need sophisticated registration logic, conditional workflows, and deep CRM integration.

### Key Features

- **Flexible registration forms** with conditional logic (skip questions based on answers)
- **Attendee data management** with duplicate detection and enrichment
- **Batch email campaigns** triggered by registration milestones
- **Badge printing and QR code check-in** for large in-person events
- **Integration with Salesforce, HubSpot, and Marketo** for lead routing
- **Compliance workflows** (GDPR, export controls, visa requirements) built-in
- **A/B testing** on registration landing pages

### Pricing

$400–$1,500+ per month. Price scales with attendee volume and custom development.

### Pros

- **Sophisticated registration logic** handles complex scenarios (team registrations, multi-tier pricing, conditional questions)
- **CRM integration** is native and bidirectional; lead data syncs automatically
- **Attendee database** is your asset; you own and can query all registration data
- **Batch operations** save time (bulk email, data exports, badge generation)
- **Support for compliance workflows** reduces manual legal/compliance work

### Cons

- **Limited user experience** compared to modern platforms; registration forms can feel dated
- **No attendee engagement features** (no mobile app, no networking, no post-event tools)
- **Steep learning curve** for non-technical users; onboarding requires 2–3 weeks
- **Pricing model** penalizes volume; large events become expensive quickly

### Best For

- Event teams running 5+ events per year
- Events requiring complex registration logic or compliance workflows
- B2B events where lead capture and CRM integration drive ROI
- Organizations building proprietary attendee databases

### Our Verdict

Swoogo is a registration powerhouse for sophisticated workflows. If you're managing compliance, complex logic, or multi-channel registration, Swoogo automates tedious tasks. The trade-off: attendee experience is basic, and you won't get engagement or networking features. Best paired with another platform (Bizzabo, Hopin) for engagement layers.

## 6. Eventbrite: Self-Service Ticketing & Event Discovery

### What It Does

Eventbrite is the world's largest self-service ticketing platform, letting organizers create events, sell tickets, and manage registrations with minimal overhead. It's dominated by creators, small venues, and indie organizers because it's free to use (or low-cost) and requires zero setup.

### Key Features

- **Free tier** (with platform fees on ticket sales) or paid tiers for advanced features
- **Event discovery** built-in; attendees browse and buy through Eventbrite's audience
- **Mobile ticket delivery** with QR codes and check-in via app
- **Email marketing** to past attendees
- **Basic analytics** showing sales, attendance, revenue
- **Integrations** with Stripe, PayPal, and other payment providers
- **Attendee data export** for CRM uploads

### Pricing

Free tier (with 2.5%+$0.99 fees per ticket), or $99–$299/month for paid organizer tiers with advanced features (custom branding, API access).

### Pros

- **Lowest barrier to entry**—completely free to list an event
- **Built-in audience**—Eventbrite's 300M+ users discover events on the platform
- **Beautiful mobile experience** attendees actually enjoy using
- **No setup complexity** create an event in 10 minutes
- **Worldwide reach** with localization and multi-currency support
- **Strong marketplace** if you want visibility (Eventbrite handles discovery)

### Cons

- **Limited customization** branding is minimal on free tier; paid tiers cost extra
- **No networking or engagement features** purely transactional
- **Not suitable for large conferences** it's a ticketing platform, not an event OS
- **Attendee data is sandboxed** harder to export and integrate with external CRMs on free tier
- **Marketplace discovery** varies by category; niche events get less visibility

### Best For

- First-time event creators
- Small-to-medium events (under 500 attendees)
- Venue-based events (fitness classes, workshops, concerts, meetups)
- Events where built-in audience and discovery are valuable

### Our Verdict

Eventbrite is the right choice if you're creating a simple, ticketed event and want zero friction. Don't come here for attendee engagement or complex workflows—that's not the mission. Ideal for creators, indie organizers, and businesses running one-off events. When you outgrow Eventbrite, migrate to Cvent or Bizzabo.

## 8. Grip: AI-Powered Networking & Matchmaking Engine

### What It Does

Grip is the AI-native option for events, automating attendee matching, scheduling, and networking through intelligent algorithms. It learns from attendee behavior and preferences to recommend meetings before they arrive at the event.

### Key Features

- **AI-powered matchmaking** predicting attendee pairs likely to meet (with 75%+ acceptance rate)
- **Pre-event networking** attendees can schedule meetings before arriving
- **Real-time recommendation engine** suggesting meetings during the event
- **Meeting scheduler** eliminating email back-and-forths
- **Post-event insights** showing which meetings drove follow-up conversations
- **Integration with Outlook and Google Calendar**
- **Mobile app** with push notifications and meeting confirmations

### Pricing

$199–$599/month depending on attendee volume. Custom enterprise pricing available.

### Pros

- **AI matching is genuinely smarter** than most platforms; acceptance rates are high
- **Pre-event networking** extends event value; relationships begin before day-one
- **Reduces friction** for busy attendees (automated scheduling vs. email hunting)
- **Measurable follow-up** shows which meetings turned into deals or partnerships
- **Lightweight setup** if you're only adding networking to an existing event

### Cons

- **Registration + engagement still required** Grip is *just* matchmaking; you'll integrate with Eventbrite, Splash, or Cvent
- **Early-stage platform** not proven at massive scale (10K+ attendee events)
- **AI requires sufficient data** works best at events with 300+ attendees
- **Pricing is steep** for smaller events; not ideal for under-200-person events

### Best For

- B2B conferences and summits where business meetings are the objective
- Events with 300+ attendees where many relationships start unknown
- Attendee bases with diverse roles (investors, founders, vendors all in same event)
- Teams layering AI networking on existing event infrastructure

### Our Verdict

Grip is the future of event networking—attendees love it because meetings *feel* natural, not algorithmically forced. If your event's success metric is "business relationships formed," Grip's ROI is measurable. Not recommended as a standalone platform; use it as a networking layer alongside registration and engagement tools. Best for B2B events at scale.

## Final Recommendations

**Choose Cvent** if: You're running a large enterprise conference with complex logistics, venue coordination, and housing management. Setup is heavy but payoff is proven.

**Choose Bizzabo** if: Your event's success hinges on attendee satisfaction and meaningful connections. Best-in-class experience and ROI analytics.

**Choose Hopin** if: You're running a virtual or hybrid event and want the easiest path to launch. Excellent value under $100/month.

**Choose Splash** if: You're marketing a small experiential event and need beautiful landing pages + simple ticketing.

**Choose Swoogo** if: You're managing multiple annual events with complex registration logic or strict compliance requirements.

**Choose Eventbrite** if: You're a first-time organizer creating a simple ticketed event and want zero friction.

**Choose Whova** if: You're layering engagement on top of existing registration infrastructure (Cvent, Swoogo, Eventbrite).

**Choose Grip** if: You're running a B2B event where attendee networking and pre-event matchmaking drive ROI.

The modern event stack often combines two platforms: a registration backbone (Cvent, Eventbrite, Swoogo) + an engagement layer (Bizzabo, Whova, Grip). Start with your event's primary success metric, then build from there.
