## Pre-Production: Research & Episode Prep

### 1. Claude — Best for Episode Research, Scripting, and Show Prep

**Price:** Free / $20/mo (Pro) / $100/mo (Max)
**Free Tier:** Yes

The difference between a forgettable podcast and one people recommend is preparation. Listeners can hear when a host has done their homework — when questions go deeper than surface-level, when examples are specific rather than generic, when the conversation has direction instead of meandering.

Claude's 200K context window is what makes it the best research tool for podcasters. Before an interview, paste your guest's LinkedIn profile, their latest articles, their previous podcast appearances, and the topic you want to explore. Claude synthesizes all of it and generates interview questions that reference specific things the guest has said or done. Your guest notices. Your audience notices.

For solo episodes, Claude is equally powerful. Give it your topic and ask for a structured outline with supporting data points, counterarguments, and real-world examples. A solo episode that would take 3 hours to research and outline takes 30 minutes. The content quality doesn't drop — it improves, because Claude surfaces angles and data you wouldn't have found in a manual research session.

Beyond episode prep, Claude handles the tedious production writing: episode descriptions, social media posts, newsletter summaries, and pitch emails to potential guests. "Write an email inviting [Guest Name] onto a podcast about [Topic], referencing their recent work on [X]" produces a personalized pitch in 30 seconds.

**Pros:**
- 200K context handles massive research inputs — guest profiles, articles, transcripts
- Generates interview questions that demonstrate real preparation
- Excellent at outlines, descriptions, and guest pitch emails
- Free tier handles 5-10 episode prep sessions per week

**Cons:**
- No web browsing — pair with Perplexity for real-time research
- Scripted episodes can sound stiff if you read Claude's output verbatim — use it as structure, not a teleprompter
- Free tier throttles during peak hours

### 2. Perplexity AI — Best for Guest Research and Trending Topics

**Price:** Free / $20/mo (Pro)
**Free Tier:** Yes — 5 Pro searches/day

Perplexity solves the part of episode prep that Claude can't: real-time information. When you're researching a guest, a trending topic, or breaking news in your niche, you need current data with sources — not a language model's training data from months ago.

Before every guest interview, search Perplexity for your guest's name plus their field. It returns a structured summary of their recent work, media appearances, and public positions — with citations you can verify. This is the difference between asking "So tell me about your background" (lazy, the guest has answered this 50 times) and "Your paper from last month argues X — how has the response been?" (specific, shows you did the work).

For topic-based episodes, Perplexity's focus modes let you search specific sources. Building an episode around a controversial industry trend? Search Reddit and Twitter to find what real people are saying — not what the official press releases claim. Creating a data-heavy episode? Search academic and business sources for credible statistics.

**Pros:**
- Every answer includes citations — verify claims before putting them on air
- Focus modes target Reddit, academic, YouTube, or general web sources
- Real-time data that Claude and ChatGPT can't match
- Free tier's 5 Pro searches/day is enough for weekly episode prep

**Cons:**
- Not as strong for creative tasks — use Claude for scripting and outlines
- Can surface outdated sources — always check publication dates
- Pro searches are limited even on the paid plan

## Production: Recording & Editing

### 3. Descript — Best All-in-One Podcast Editor

**Price:** Free / $24/mo (Hobbyist) / $33/mo (Pro)
**Free Tier:** Yes — 1 hour of transcription

Descript changed podcast editing the way Google Docs changed word processing. Instead of scrubbing through a waveform and cutting audio by timestamp, you edit a text transcript. Delete a sentence from the transcript, and Descript removes it from the audio. Rearrange paragraphs, and the audio follows. If you can edit a document, you can edit a podcast.

The filler word removal alone justifies the subscription. Descript automatically detects and removes "um," "uh," "like," "you know," and other verbal tics. For a typical 60-minute interview, this removes 3-5 minutes of filler and makes both host and guest sound more polished. Manually doing this in Audacity would take 90 minutes. Descript does it in one click.

Studio Sound, their AI audio enhancement feature, is the other standout. It normalizes volume levels, reduces background noise, and applies broadcast-quality processing to recordings made on consumer microphones. A guest calling from their kitchen on AirPods sounds significantly closer to a guest in a treated studio. It's not magic — a bad mic is still a bad mic — but it closes the gap enough that the audio quality doesn't distract from the conversation.

Descript also handles multitrack editing for remote recordings, screen recording for video podcasts, and clip creation for social media. It's the closest thing to a one-stop production suite for podcasters.

**Pros:**
- Text-based editing eliminates waveform scrubbing — edit audio like a document
- One-click filler word removal saves hours per episode
- Studio Sound makes consumer-grade recordings sound professional
- Built-in clip creation for social media repurposing

**Cons:**
- Free tier's 1-hour limit means you need to upgrade quickly
- AI transcription occasionally misidentifies speakers in multi-guest shows
- Heavy project files can slow down older machines
- Learning curve for advanced features (multitrack, video)

## Post-Production: Transcription & Repurposing

### 5. Otter.ai — Best for Automated Transcription and Show Notes

**Price:** Free / $17/mo (Pro) / $40/mo (Business)
**Free Tier:** Yes — 300 minutes/month

Show notes are the SEO engine of podcasting. Google can't index audio, but it can index a well-structured transcript with headers, timestamps, and key takeaways. Podcasters who publish transcripts get 2-3x more organic search traffic than those who publish a title and a "click play" embed. Otter.ai makes transcription effortless.

Upload your episode audio and Otter returns a timestamped, speaker-labeled transcript within minutes. Accuracy is consistently above 95% for clear English audio — good enough to publish with light editing rather than a full rewrite. For interviews, the automatic speaker labeling means you don't have to manually tag "Host:" and "Guest:" throughout a 60-minute conversation.

The AI-generated summaries are surprisingly useful for show notes. Otter identifies key topics, action items, and notable quotes from the conversation. For a weekly podcast, this replaces 30-45 minutes of manual show note writing per episode. Over a year, that's 25+ hours reclaimed — a full work week you can spend creating content instead of documenting it.

The free tier at 300 minutes per month covers about 5 hour-long episodes — enough for most weekly shows. Only upgrade when you're producing daily or running multiple shows.

**Pros:**
- 95%+ transcription accuracy for clear audio
- Automatic speaker labeling for interview shows
- AI-generated summaries double as show notes
- Free tier (300 min/month) covers most weekly podcasts

**Cons:**
- Accuracy drops with heavy accents, overlapping speakers, or poor audio
- Speaker identification needs manual correction for 3+ person shows
- Summary feature occasionally misidentifies the most important topics
- No direct podcast hosting integration — export and paste

## Growth: Audience Building & Repurposing

### 8. ChatGPT — Best for Turning Episodes into Multi-Platform Content

**Price:** Free / $20/mo (Plus) / $200/mo (Pro)
**Free Tier:** Yes

A single podcast episode contains enough raw content for a week of multi-platform marketing — if you can extract it efficiently. Most podcasters record, publish, share one link, and move on. ChatGPT turns that one episode into a blog post, a newsletter, a Twitter thread, LinkedIn articles, and Instagram captions, each formatted for its platform.

The workflow is straightforward. Paste your episode transcript into ChatGPT and ask for specific outputs. "Turn this transcript into a 1,500-word blog post optimized for the keyword [X]" produces a publish-ready draft. "Extract 10 tweetable quotes from this conversation" gives you a week of social content. "Write a newsletter summary that makes readers want to listen to the full episode" generates your email in 60 seconds.

What makes ChatGPT better than Claude for repurposing is its understanding of platform-specific formatting. It knows that LinkedIn posts perform better with line breaks every 2-3 sentences, that Twitter threads need hooks in the first tweet, and that Instagram captions should frontload the value proposition. Claude's output tends to be more analytical — ChatGPT's is more social-native.

For podcasters who monetize through sponsorships, ChatGPT also writes host-read ad scripts that match your show's tone. "Write a 60-second ad read for [Product] in the style of my show — casual, conversational, slightly sarcastic" produces something that sounds like you wrote it, not like you read a PDF the sponsor emailed.

**Pros:**
- Turns one transcript into blog posts, newsletters, social content, and ad scripts
- Strong understanding of platform-specific formatting (Twitter threads, LinkedIn, Instagram)
- Free tier handles most repurposing workflows
- GPT-4o voice mode can even help with pronunciation prep for ad reads

**Cons:**
- Repurposed content needs your editorial voice layered on top — raw output sounds generic
- Can hallucinate details if the transcript is unclear — always fact-check
- Long transcripts may need to be split into chunks on the free tier
- Not as strong as Claude for deep research or analytical content
