## 1. Jane App — Best All-in-One Practice Management

Jane App is the de facto standard for allied health practices built after 2018. It was designed from the ground up for chiropractors, physical therapists, massage therapists, and naturopaths—not retrofitted from a physician EHR template. The platform handles appointment scheduling, patient intake, clinical notes, invoicing, and payment processing in a single unified interface. For practices moving from paper or outdated systems, Jane eliminates the need for four separate subscriptions.

The clinical workflow is intuitive: check-in happens on a patient-facing iPad, historical notes auto-populate during the appointment, and templated SOAP note scaffolding cuts documentation time by 50% compared to free-form writing. Insurance billing is handled automatically for most major providers, and the mobile app lets clinicians document during off-hours. Jane's integration ecosystem is smaller than competitors, but Zapier support means you can connect it to virtually any downstream tool (CRM, email marketing, patient communication platforms).

**Pros:**
- Purpose-built for allied health practices, not a physician EHR compromised for your specialty
- Fastest onboarding—most practices go live in 2-3 weeks
- Transparent pricing (no hidden per-transaction or integration fees)
- Strong security certifications (SOC 2, HIPAA-compliant, encrypted at rest and in transit)
- Excellent customer support with live chat during business hours and a robust knowledge base
- Built-in patient communication (secure messaging, appointment reminders via SMS/email)

**Cons:**
- Limited ambient AI (no auto-SOAP generation; you still write notes from templates)
- Zapier integration can feel clunky for complex workflows compared to native API access
- Higher price point than some single-feature competitors when multi-location practices scale
- Limited inventory/supply tracking compared to specialized modules in ChiroTouch
- Longer contract terms (annual preferred; month-to-month available at premium)
- Reporting suite not as granular as practice management systems aimed at large groups

**Bottom Line:** Jane App is the right choice if you want a single integrated platform that just works, with excellent UX and no surprises. It's ideal for practices 1-15 providers that don't need heavy custom integrations. If your practice is already in a different EHR and you're happy with it, Jane won't justify a migration. But for new practices or those on outdated systems, Jane saves 6-8 hours/week compared to managing separate scheduling, billing, and documentation tools.

**Pros:**

Here's how it works: Freed listens to your conversation with the patient, identifies clinical concepts (chief complaint, vital signs mentioned, physical findings, treatment provided), structures them into SOAP format, and pre-fills your EHR note template. You review in 2-3 minutes, make edits, and sign. Practices report 60-75% time savings on note writing. For a PT or chiropractor spending 45 minutes per day on documentation, that's 25-30 minutes reclaimed daily. Freed's AI is trained on physical rehabilitation encounter patterns, not just physician visits, which means the note drafts are relevant to your specialty.

The HIPAA compliance is rigorous: audio is encrypted end-to-end, processed on compliant servers, and automatically deleted after note generation. Freed has SOC 2 Type II certification and works with 9,000+ clinicians across allied health.

## Implementation Roadmap: How to Roll Out AI in Your Practice

### Phase 1: Foundation (Weeks 1-2)
**If you don't have an EHR:** Start with Jane App or ChiroTouch. Deploy to one provider first as a pilot, then expand to the rest of the team after 2 weeks. This prevents team disruption and gives you time to refine workflows.

**If you already have an EHR:** Add Freed AI immediately. It works with your existing system and delivers ROI within days. Most practices see 6-8 hours/week reclaimed in Phase 1.

**Goals:** Get one provider live on the EHR or AI tool, document workflows on paper, identify biggest pain points.

### Phase 2: Team Expansion (Weeks 3-6)
Roll out the EHR or AI tool to your full provider team. Run back-to-back training sessions (30 minutes per session, max 3 providers at a time). Assign a "go-to" person on your team to answer questions and troubleshoot login issues.

**For EHR implementations:** Dedicate a staff member to data migration (patient history, appointment schedules). This is the bottleneck for most rollouts.

**For AI documentation:** Record sample patient visits for the AI to learn your clinic's patterns and terminology. This improves accuracy by 10-15% over the first 100 notes.

**Goals:** All providers documenting in new system with <20% longer documentation time than before.

### Phase 3: Optimization (Weeks 7-12)
Run retrospective analysis: Which providers are using the system most effectively? Which workflows are still creating friction? Adjust templates, build shortcuts, and automate common actions (e.g., auto-fill insurance info on new patient forms).

**If using Freed or AICA:** Begin analyzing note quality and accuracy. Flag common mistakes and feed back to the AI (most platforms have feedback mechanisms that improve model accuracy).

**Add a second tool:** If you've stabilized on your EHR, now is the time to add PatientPop (patient acquisition) or Cliniko (if multi-discipline).

**Goals:** Workflow efficiency back to baseline speed; team confidence in new systems; 5-10 hours/week reclaimed.

### Phase 4: Scale (Weeks 13+)
If you've successfully implemented Phase 1-3 and team adoption is strong, expand AI tooling. Add Freed AI if you implemented ChiroTouch; add ChiroTouch integrations if you're on Jane App; invest in marketing automation if patient acquisition is the constraint.

Measure outcomes: track new patient volume, documentation time per visit, claim approval speed, patient satisfaction scores. Use these metrics to justify budget for additional team members or providers.
