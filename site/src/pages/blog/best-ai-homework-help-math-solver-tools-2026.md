## What to Look For in an AI Homework Tool

Before the reviews, five things separate AI homework tools that actually teach from ones that just do your homework for you:

1. **Teaching mode vs. answer mode** — The most important distinction in 2026. Tools like Khanmigo and ChatGPT Study Mode use Socratic questioning: they ask you guiding questions, give hints, and walk you toward the answer rather than handing it to you. Tools like Mathway and Photomath default to showing the full solution. Neither approach is wrong — sometimes you need to learn, sometimes you need to check your work — but know which mode you're getting before you pay
2. **Step-by-step solution quality** — Every tool claims "step-by-step solutions." The real test is whether each step explains the *why*, not just the *what*. "Distribute 3 across (x + 2)" is better than "3x + 6." Symbolab and Wolfram Alpha show the mathematical reasoning. Cheaper tools often skip intermediate steps or explain them so tersely that you can't follow the logic
3. **Subject coverage** — Math-only tools (Photomath, Symbolab) go deep in mathematics but can't help with chemistry, physics, or writing. All-subject tools (Khanmigo, Brainly, ChatGPT) handle broader homework but may give less rigorous math solutions. If you're struggling specifically in calculus, a math-specialized tool is worth the narrower scope
4. **Camera scanning accuracy** — Scanning handwritten problems is the killer feature for math apps. Photomath and Gauth lead here — they handle messy handwriting, multi-line equations, and word problems from textbook photos. Tools that only accept typed input (Wolfram Alpha, ChatGPT) add friction when you're working from a physical worksheet or textbook
5. **Academic integrity features** — This matters more than you think. Schools are increasingly monitoring AI tool usage, and some tools are designed to leave an audit trail. Khanmigo is built for classroom use — teachers can see what students asked and how the AI responded. ChatGPT Study Mode explicitly avoids giving direct answers. If your school has an AI policy, check whether the tool you're considering aligns with it

## 3. Wolfram Alpha — Best for Advanced Math & STEM

**Price:** Free (basic) · Pro: $9.99/mo or $60/yr · Pro Premium: $12/mo or $99/yr · Student discounts available (~30% off via UNiDAYS)

**Platforms:** Web, iOS, Android

[Wolfram Alpha](/tools/wolfram-alpha) isn't an AI homework helper in the way the other tools on this list are. It's a computational knowledge engine — a system that actually *computes* answers using curated algorithms and data, rather than predicting them with a language model. When you ask it to integrate a function, it runs the integration using Mathematica's symbolic computation engine. When ChatGPT integrates a function, it's pattern-matching against training data and sometimes gets it wrong. For advanced math, this distinction is critical.

**What makes it stand out:**
- **Computational accuracy** — Wolfram Alpha doesn't guess. It computes. For calculus, linear algebra, differential equations, statistics, and discrete math, the answers are mathematically verified, not probabilistically generated. This matters when you're past the level where ChatGPT reliably produces correct solutions
- **Step-by-step solutions (Pro)** — Pro subscribers get detailed solution breakdowns for most problem types, including intermediate steps that show the mathematical reasoning. The depth is comparable to a textbook solution manual
- **Practice problems** — Pro generates practice problems similar to what you just solved, with guided hints. Useful for exam prep when you've run out of textbook problems
- **Wolfram Notebook Assistant** — Released December 2024, this lets you describe what you want to compute in plain English and generates Wolfram Language code to execute it. Bridges the gap between "I know what I need to calculate" and "I don't know the syntax"
- **ChatGPT integration** — Wolfram GPT (a Custom GPT) brings Wolfram Alpha's computation into ChatGPT conversations. Ask ChatGPT to solve a problem and it can call Wolfram Alpha for the computation, giving you natural language explanation plus verified math

**What could be better:**
- The interface is functional, not friendly. It's built for people who know what they're looking for, not students who aren't sure where to start. Typing "solve 3x + 5 = 20" works perfectly; typing "I don't understand fractions" does not
- No camera scanning — you must type or paste problems. For students working from a physical worksheet, this adds friction compared to Photomath or Gauth
- The free tier is increasingly limited. Basic queries return results, but step-by-step solutions, practice problems, and extended computation time all require Pro
- Covers STEM subjects deeply but doesn't help with humanities, writing, or social sciences

**Who it's for:** The non-negotiable tool for college STEM students. If you're in engineering, physics, statistics, or any math-heavy program, Wolfram Alpha Pro is worth the $5/month (with student discount). It's not a tutor — it's a computational reference that happens to explain its work. Pair it with Khanmigo or ChatGPT Study Mode for the teaching component.

## FAQ

### Are AI homework tools cheating?

It depends on how you use them — and your school's policy. A 2026 survey found that 92% of students use AI while studying, but only 18% use it to fully complete assignments. Using Khanmigo or ChatGPT Study Mode to understand a concept you're stuck on is analogous to asking a tutor — most schools allow it. Using Photomath to copy 30 answers you never looked at is plagiarism by any definition. The practical test: if you can solve a similar problem without the tool after using it, you learned something. If you can't, you copied. Check your school's specific AI policy — many now distinguish between "AI as tutor" (allowed) and "AI as author" (prohibited).

### Can AI math solvers handle advanced topics like differential equations or linear algebra?

Wolfram Alpha handles these reliably because it *computes* rather than *predicts* — its symbolic math engine is the same one used by professional mathematicians and engineers. ChatGPT and Gemini can handle many advanced problems but make errors on edge cases, especially with multi-step proofs, unusual boundary conditions, and symbolic manipulation of complex expressions. For anything past Calc I, verify ChatGPT's math answers against Wolfram Alpha. Photomath and Mathway handle calculus and some linear algebra but struggle with proof-based problems and abstract algebra.

### Which tool is best for elementary and middle school kids?

Khanmigo is the clear winner for younger students. The Socratic approach prevents answer-copying, the curriculum aligns with K-12 standards, and the $4/month family plan covers up to 10 children. For parents who want their kids to learn independently, Khanmigo's refusal to just give answers is a feature, not a limitation. Photomath is a reasonable second choice for checking math homework, but it shows full solutions — which means kids can copy without learning if unsupervised. Avoid Brainly and Gauth for young kids — the community aspects and advertising are designed for older students.

### What happened to Microsoft Math Solver and Socratic?

Both are gone. Microsoft officially discontinued Math Solver in July 2025, shutting down the mobile apps and math.microsoft.com. The functionality was absorbed into Microsoft Copilot, which offers general math help but not as a dedicated solver. Socratic by Google was retired around 2024, with its camera-based homework features folded into Google Lens. Many "best tools 2026" articles still list both as active products — they're not. If you were using either, Photomath (camera scanning) and ChatGPT Study Mode (conversational help) are the closest replacements.

### Should I worry about Chegg's financial troubles affecting Mathway and Symbolab?

It's worth monitoring. Chegg's stock has fallen 99% from its 2021 peak, the company cut 45% of its workforce in October 2025, and it's actively suing Google. Both Mathway and Symbolab work fine today — the apps are stable, the solutions are accurate, and there's been no degradation in service. But Chegg's revenue dropped more than a third year-over-year, and the company's long-term viability is an open question. Practical advice: if you're a paying subscriber to either tool, export or screenshot any saved solution history periodically. If Chegg restructures or sells these products, your data may not transfer. For new subscribers, the tools are still worth using — just be aware of the corporate context and have a backup plan (ChatGPT Study Mode + Wolfram Alpha covers most of what these tools do).
