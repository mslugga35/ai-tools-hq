## What to Look For in an AI Caption Tool

Before the reviews, five things separate caption tools that save you hours from ones that create more work than they eliminate:

1. **Accuracy on your content type** — A tool that's 95% accurate on clear podcast audio may drop to 80% on outdoor vlogs with background noise, multiple speakers, or accented English. The tools that handle real-world audio well (Happy Scribe, Rev, Descript) use speaker diarization and noise cancellation. The ones that struggle (free tiers of most tools) assume clean single-speaker audio. If you're captioning interviews, panels, or any multi-speaker content, accuracy on your specific format matters more than a headline accuracy number

2. **Export format support** — SRT and VTT are the two universal subtitle formats, but not every tool exports both. If you're uploading to YouTube, either works. If you're burning captions into video for Instagram Reels or TikTok, you need hardcoded/burned-in support. If you're working in professional post-production, you need export to Final Cut Pro XML, Adobe Premiere Pro sequences, or DaVinci Resolve timelines. Check what your destination requires before choosing a tool

3. **Caption styling and animation** — For social media, plain white text on a black bar is invisible. The tools winning on TikTok and Instagram (CapCut, Kapwing, VEED) offer word-by-word highlighting, karaoke-style animations, custom fonts, color gradients, and emoji integration. For corporate or broadcast use, you want clean, readable, ADA-compliant captions — no animation, proper contrast ratios, configurable font sizes. These are fundamentally different styling needs

4. **Language and translation support** — Generating captions in the original language is table stakes. The real differentiator is translation quality. Tools like VEED and Happy Scribe support 100-150+ languages for both transcription and translation. But translated captions still need review — AI translation accuracy drops significantly for idiomatic speech, technical jargon, and languages with fewer training data. If multilingual captions are critical to your workflow, test the specific language pair you need before committing

5. **Pricing model** — Per-minute pricing (Rev, Sonix) rewards low-volume users and punishes heavy ones. Monthly subscriptions with minute caps (Happy Scribe, VEED) reward consistent creators. Unlimited plans (CapCut Pro, Kapwing Pro) reward high-volume users but cost more upfront. Calculate your actual monthly captioning volume — in minutes of audio — and compare the effective cost per minute across tools. A $17/month plan with 120 minutes is $0.14/min. A $10/hour pay-as-you-go plan is $0.17/min. The cheapest-looking plan isn't always the cheapest per minute

## FAQ

### Do I need captions if I only post on YouTube?

Yes — and not just for accessibility. YouTube's algorithm uses caption text to understand video content, which affects search rankings and suggested video placement. Videos with accurate captions rank higher for relevant search queries than videos with auto-generated or missing captions. YouTube's built-in auto-captions are free and reach 85-95% accuracy on clear English audio, so the baseline is already there. But uploading your own corrected captions (SRT file) tells the algorithm exactly what your video covers, removes the errors that auto-captions inevitably introduce, and provides a better experience for the 70% of viewers who watch with captions on.

### Are AI-generated captions good enough for ADA/FCC compliance?

It depends on the accuracy and your specific compliance context. The FCC's closed-captioning rules require captions that are accurate, synchronous, complete, and properly placed. AI-generated captions at 85-95% accuracy may not meet the "accurate" standard for broadcast content — a 5-15% error rate means multiple errors per minute of content. For web-only content, ADA compliance requires "effective communication," which courts have increasingly interpreted as requiring captions on video content. Best practice: use AI to generate the first draft, then review and correct errors before publishing. For broadcast or legal compliance, use human-reviewed captions (Happy Scribe at $2/min or Rev at $1.99/min).

### What's the difference between open captions and closed captions?

Closed captions are a separate file (SRT, VTT) that viewers can toggle on or off. Open captions (also called burned-in or hardcoded captions) are permanently embedded in the video — viewers can't turn them off. YouTube, Vimeo, and most video platforms support closed captions via uploaded SRT/VTT files. Social media platforms (Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn) play videos on mute in feeds, so burned-in open captions are the standard — there's no caption toggle in a social feed. Tools like CapCut, Kapwing, and VEED specialize in open captions. Tools like Happy Scribe, Rev, and Sonix specialize in closed captions. Some tools (VEED, Descript) handle both.

### How accurate are AI captions on non-English content?

Accuracy drops significantly outside of English, Spanish, French, German, and Portuguese — the languages with the most training data. For major European and East Asian languages, expect 80-90% accuracy on clear audio. For less-common languages, accuracy can drop below 70%. Happy Scribe and Rev have the widest language support and generally the best non-English accuracy because they've invested specifically in multilingual models. CapCut and Zubtitle are weaker on non-English content. For any non-English captioning, test with a sample video in your specific language before committing to a paid plan — the accuracy variance between tools on the same language can be 20+ percentage points.

### Can I use these tools for live captioning or real-time subtitles?

Most tools on this list are designed for pre-recorded content — you upload a finished video and receive captions after processing. Live captioning is a different technical challenge requiring real-time speech recognition with sub-second latency. Rev offers live captioning services (human and AI). VEED has a live streaming feature with real-time captions. For dedicated live captioning, look at specialized tools like Otter.ai (meetings), Wordly (events and conferences), or Web Captioner (free browser-based). The pre-recorded tools on this list won't help during a live broadcast or meeting — they're designed for post-production captioning.
