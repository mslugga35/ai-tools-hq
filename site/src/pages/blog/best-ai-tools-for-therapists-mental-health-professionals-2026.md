## 1. Mentalyc — Best AI Session Notes

**Price:** $49–$99/month (per therapist)  
**Best for:** Solo practitioners, small group practices, documentation-heavy workflows

Mentalyc does one thing exceptionally well: it listens to your therapy sessions and writes your clinical notes for you. With patient consent, the app records your session and transcribes it. You then feed that transcription (or a written summary) to Mentalyc's AI, which generates a full SOAP note, DAP note, or progress note in seconds — formatted to your specific style and EHR system.

The workflow is dead simple. After a session, you open Mentalyc, paste the session transcript or summary, select your note format (SOAP, DAP, progress note, case conceptualization), and pick a tone — "clinical," "formal," "conversational," etc. Mentalyc generates a first draft. You review it in 3-5 minutes, make edits, and save it directly to your EHR or copy it out.

What matters: Mentalyc saves 20-25 minutes per note. For a therapist seeing 20 clients per week, that's 6-8 hours reclaimed. Mentalyc holds a signed BAA (Business Associate Agreement), encrypts all patient data at rest and in transit, and never uses your data to train its models. The company underwent a SOC 2 Type II audit in 2025. Sessions are recorded on your device — no upload to Mentalyc's servers unless you explicitly save the note.

**Pros:**
- Saves 70-80% of documentation time
- Maintains HIPAA compliance with signed BAA
- Works with all EHR systems (copies/exports as needed)
- No contract lock-in; month-to-month billing
- Free tier available for limited notes

**Cons:**
- Requires explicit patient consent for recording

**Bottom line:** If your documentation workload is crushing you and you need the fastest ROI, Mentalyc is the most cost-effective option. Plan on 15 minutes per note for review; the initial draft does 70% of the work.

## 2. Blueprint Health — Best Outcome Measurement

**Price:** $50–$150/month per provider  
**Best for:** Evidence-based therapists, practices tracking treatment outcomes, outcome-accountable billing

Blueprint Health tackles the measurement problem. Therapy outcomes matter — they determine whether treatment is working, inform adjusted interventions, and increasingly, drive insurance reimbursement. But therapists historically haven't had an easy way to track this. Blueprint automates outcome measurement, asking clients brief validated questionnaires (PHQ-9, GAD-7, PCL-5, etc.) before or between sessions, then dashboards the results for your clinical review.

Here's the workflow: Your patient gets a text link or email with a 2-3 minute questionnaire. They complete it on their phone. Blueprint scores it, flags clinically significant change, and surfaces it in your dashboard before your next session. You can see progress trends over months. The system also generates clinical reports you can share with patients — making the data visible motivates behavior change.

What makes this clinically relevant: Blueprint uses validated instruments recognized by insurance companies, licensing boards, and outcome research. The data is yours; you control it. Blueprint maintains HIPAA compliance, signs BAAs, and encrypts all patient data.

**Pros:**
- Uses evidence-based questionnaires (PHQ-9, GAD-7, PCL-5, CORE-OM, etc.)
- Tracks progress trends over time — shows what actually works
- Patient portal and shared reports drive engagement
- Automated alerts for safety (suicidal ideation, clinical deterioration)
- Integrates with major EHRs

**Cons:**
- Requires patient participation in questionnaires (some don't respond)
- Steeper learning curve than documentation tools
- Pricing jumps with group size; solo practitioners at lower tier

**Bottom line:** If you practice outcome-informed therapy or need to satisfy insurance requirements around progress tracking, Blueprint is gold. It's the bridge between "did the patient feel better?" and "can we prove the treatment worked?"

TherapyNotes is built by people who understand that world. The tool is less flashy than SimplePractice but more specialized. HIPAA compliance is standard.

**Pros:**

## HIPAA Compliance: What Every Therapist Must Know

All eight tools in this review maintain HIPAA compliance. But compliance is a shared responsibility. Here's what you need to do:

1. **Request and verify the BAA** — Before signing up, ask the vendor for a signed Business Associate Agreement. If they won't sign one, don't use the tool for patient data. All eight tools here will sign.

2. **Check the SOC 2 audit** — Reputable vendors undergo annual SOC 2 Type II audits. Request a copy or at least confirmation that one exists. This verifies their security controls are externally validated.

3. **Understand where data lives** — Where are patient records stored? In the vendor's cloud? Your own server? Who has access? Know the answer before onboarding.

4. **Never paste patient data into non-HIPAA tools** — ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, or any free LLM: do not paste psychotherapy notes there. The data is not encrypted, not covered by BAA, and potentially used to train the model. Use tools specifically designed for healthcare.

5. **Configure access controls** — Even if the tool is HIPAA-compliant, make sure only you (and authorized staff) have login access. Use strong passwords, enable 2FA, audit login logs.
