<h2>Transcription quality in 2026: how to evaluate accuracy beyond marketing claims</h2>
<p>Distributed teams run meetings with different accents, audio setups, and speaker overlap. That’s why transcription quality matters more than most AI features.</p>

<p>When teams compare <strong>Best AI Meeting Assistants for Distributed Teams 2026</strong>, they should understand what “accuracy” really means. A common framing is Word Error Rate (WER), where <strong>95% accuracy implies ~5% WER</strong> (about 1 in 20 words wrong).</p>

<p>So instead of asking “Is it 90% accurate?”, ask “Does it preserve the details my team needs?” For example, names, decisions, deadlines, product terms, and technical requirements.</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Reality check</strong>: even strong transcription can still require lightweight human review for critical entities.</li>
  <li><strong>Distributed-team focus</strong>: test multi-speaker overlap and meetings with background noise.</li>
  <li><strong>Action items output</strong>: verify that the summary doesn’t miss owners, commitments, or next steps.</li>
</ul>

<blockquote>
  <p><strong>Practical tip:</strong> Run one 30-minute call through any candidate tool and then check the “action items” section. If owners and deadlines are incomplete, the transcript may be “good enough” but the workflow is not.</p>
</blockquote>
